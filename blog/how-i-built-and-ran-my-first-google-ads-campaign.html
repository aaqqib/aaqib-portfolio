--- title: "How I Built and Ran My First Google Ads Campaign — And What I'd Do Differently" date: "2026-05-26" category: "Paid Ads" description: "A real-world case study of my first end-to-end Google Ads campaign for Thrillop Journeys: spending ₹1,250 over 4 days, achieving a 14% CTR, facing zero-impression & zero-lead problems, and critical landing page lessons learned." --- Every marketer remembers their first live campaign. The mix of excitement and mild anxiety when you hit "Publish" and real budget starts leaving the account. Recently, I got the opportunity to run my first end-to-end Google Ads campaign. No templates, no senior oversight. Just the account, the client brief, and figuring it out in real time. Here is the honest case study of what happened, what worked, what failed, and what I would do differently next time. --- ## The Brief * **Client:** Thrillop Journeys — a Kashmir-based travel company offering customised tour packages * **Role:** SEO & Digital Marketing Executive * **Campaign Duration:** 23–26 May 2026 * **Total Budget Spent:** ₹1,250 * **Tools Used:** Google Ads, Google Tag Manager, Google Analytics Thrillop Journeys had an SEO-optimised website and a solid package catalogue but zero paid traffic history. The goal was straightforward — run a Google Search campaign targeting people actively searching for Kashmir tour packages, and generate enquiry leads. --- ## The Setup: Audience-First Ad Groups Before writing a single ad, I mapped out the audience and intent. People searching for Kashmir tours fall into three distinct buckets — general travellers, honeymooners, and families. Each group searches differently and expects different things from a landing page. So instead of dumping all keywords into one ad group, I built three tightly themed ad groups: 1. **Kashmir Tour Packages** 2. **Kashmir Honeymoon Packages** 3. **Kashmir Family Tour Packages** Each ad group got its own Responsive Search Ad with 8–10 headlines written specifically for that audience: * The **honeymoon ad** talked about Shikara rides and snow-capped mountains. * The **family ad** talked about safety and kid-friendly itineraries. * The **general ad** led with price and inclusions. For match types, I used **Phrase Match** across the board — broad enough to capture relevant variations, tight enough to avoid irrelevant traffic at a limited budget of ₹300/day. --- ## The First Problem: Zero Impressions for 24 Hours After launching, the campaign sat completely silent. No impressions, no clicks, nothing — despite all ads showing as *Eligible*. The culprit was the bidding strategy. I had set **Maximize Conversions**, which Google recommends for lead generation campaigns. What the interface doesn't make obvious is that Maximize Conversions requires conversion history to function effectively. On a brand new account with zero historical data, the algorithm has nothing to optimise toward — so it bids extremely conservatively and loses every auction without spending a rupee. **The Fix:** I switched the campaign to **Manual CPC** and set bids of ₹25–30 per keyword. Within hours of switching, the campaign generated **275 impressions and 29 clicks** in a single day.

💡 Lesson learned: Automated bidding strategies are powerful but need data to work. On a new account, always start with Manual CPC and build conversion history first.

The average CPC of **₹23.15** against a ₹40–80 benchmark for travel keywords meant the campaign was winning auctions efficiently. --- ## The Second Problem: Zero Leads 54 clicks. ₹1,250 spent. Zero enquiries. This is the uncomfortable truth of this case study — and also the most valuable learning. High CTR with zero conversions always points to one place: **the landing page**. After auditing thrillop.com on mobile, I identified several issues: 1. **No enquiry path above the fold:** On mobile, visitors landed on a package page and saw the itinerary immediately. The only way to enquire was a small "Get a Quote" button buried below the content. Most visitors never scrolled that far. 2. **Message mismatch on one ad group:** The top-performing keyword "kashmir travel packages" was sending traffic to a specific *5 Nights 6 Days* package page instead of the main packages listing. Someone searching generically expects to browse options — not land on a single predetermined package. 3. **No WhatsApp CTA:** For Indian travel buyers, WhatsApp is the primary enquiry channel. The page had a phone number in the header but no tap-to-WhatsApp button. Adding "Get Price on WhatsApp" as a primary CTA would likely have converted several of those 54 clicks. 4. **Weak CTA copy:** "Enquire" is passive. "Get Free Quote on WhatsApp" sets a clear, low-commitment expectation and converts significantly better. I added an Enquire button above the fold mid-campaign, but the WhatsApp CTA and message mismatch fixes were identified too late to measure impact before the budget paused. --- ## What I'd Do Differently 1. **Audit the landing page before spending a rupee:** The campaign setup was solid. The conversion path wasn't. In hindsight, 30 minutes reviewing the mobile experience before launch would have changed the outcome. 2. **Add a WhatsApp CTA on day one:** For travel brands targeting Indian audiences, WhatsApp enquiries often outnumber form fills 3 to 1. It should have been the primary CTA from the start. 3. **Secure consistent budget before launching:** The campaign ran at ₹300–500/day in fragmented top-ups. Budget exhausting mid-day meant ads went silent for hours at a time. Google Ads rewards consistent spend — a steady ₹300/day for 30 days would outperform the same total spent in 4 interrupted days every time. 4. **Set up conversion tracking verification before launch:** GTM was installed and a conversion action was created, but I never verified the tag was firing correctly on form submissions. With zero tracked conversions, it's impossible to know whether the 54 clicks generated zero leads or whether leads came in untracked. --- ## What This Campaign Proved The core advertising fundamentals worked. Keyword-to-ad relevance was strong, evidenced by a 14% CTR. The CPC was efficient. The search terms report showed genuine buying intent — people searching *"kashmir tour package from Delhi"*, *"kashmir honeymoon package"*, *"bhopal to kashmir package"*. These are not informational searchers — these are people with travel plans looking for a company to book with. The gap was entirely in what happened after the click. That's the next thing to fix. --- ## Current Status The campaign is paused pending client budget confirmation. Landing page CTA improvements are identified and ready to implement. When the campaign resumes, the first 30 days with consistent budget and a fixed conversion path should produce the lead data needed to optimise further. --- *Written by **Aaqib Bhat** — SEO & Digital Marketing Executive at Insights Marketing & Communication.
